## 1. The core loop

### The loop in one picture

1. **Look at Lagos.** The city, seen from above like a model town. Dark blocks have a problem: potholes, lamps out, slow internet, a go-slow, a flooded corner.
2. **Tap a problem.** A card says what is wrong, what the fix costs and which challenge helps: "Lamps are out here. Run the lane run to collect Power." (A real place name never sits in a line that says something is wrong; see "Words".)
3. **Play the challenge.** A street run, the hall, or the lane run.
4. **Mission complete.** After a win, the hero gets off the bus in that part of the city and the fix plays out around them.
5. **The city booms.** Each fix fills the Booming meter. At each mark Lagos grows, and a growing Lagos needs new things: new problems show up somewhere else. Back to step 1.

### Three buckets: Naira, Power and Tech (Tp, L1, 10 Nov)

Everything you collect goes into one of three buckets. Each fix costs its own kind. This replaces "any Naira fixes anything" (city choice 7); the rest of choice 7 stays: no timers, no energy, nothing breaks while you are away, nothing to buy, never sold for real money.

| Bucket | Pickup in a run (our own drawing) | Shown as | Pays for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Naira** | A round coin worth 10 kobo | "80 kobo", "₦3.40" (N1 to N4) | Potholes, bus stops, markets, drains: things you build or repair |
| **Power** | A glowing bulb with a small lightning bolt | "Power 12" | Lights out, lamps, traffic lights |
| **Tech** | A small chip with signal bars over it | "Tech 5" | Slow internet, a phone mast, a skills hub |

Never colour alone: each bucket has its own icon and its own word, everywhere it shows.

Where each comes from in slice 3:
- Street runs and the hall give Naira only (as built).
- The lane run gives all three.
- Things you bump or land on in the lane run (crates, junk piles, old broken signs) burst and pop out values (2B).

### How the money adds up (first numbers, to be tuned in play)

- A street run gives the coins you pick up (about ₦6 to ₦15) plus a ₦5 win prize.
- A lane run to the goal gives about ₦4 to ₦10, Power 8 to 14 and Tech 5 to 9, plus a win prize of ₦5, Power 4 and Tech 2. Keep running for more.
- The hall gives ₦15 for a win.
- Fixes in slice 3: Potholes ₦10, Lights out Power 10, Slow internet Tech 6. Tuned so one win always pays for the fix you came for (see question 3).
- Losing a lane run keeps everything you collected (L3). Street runs: see question 2.

### The city view: Lagos from above (Tp, L4, 10 Nov)

- **What it looks like.** Lagos seen from above at an angle, like a model town on a table. Roads, blocks of buildings, the lagoon and our own bridges, yellow buses on the roads. Drawn our own way in code, in the same bright style as the streets, never a real building, bridge or landmark drawn to look real (Q22 rule 6).
- **Look around.** Drag with one finger to move; pinch to zoom in and out (two zoom steps, near and far). Keys: arrows move, + and − zoom. Double tap a block to zoom on it. Nothing else on this screen needs two fingers, so one-hand play still works.
- **Dark and lit blocks.** A block with a problem is dim, with its pin floating over it. A fixed block lights up: windows glow, lamps come on, people and buses come back. Not colour alone: dark blocks have a pin with an icon and a word, fixed blocks have no pin and a small "Fixed ✓" when you tap them.
- **Pins.** A round sign over the spot, with an icon, one word under it ("Potholes", "Lights out", "Slow internet", "Go-slow", "Shadow") and a gentle bob. When you have enough in the right bucket, the pin gets a tick badge and the word "Ready".
- **Slice 3 shows one area** (around Ikeja: Alausa, Computer Village, Ikeja). Later areas join at the edges (the island, Ikorodu, Badagry, Epe and more, each name checked with the Q22 rule).
- **Nothing goes wrong on its own.** A fixed block never breaks again on a timer. No waiting clocks, no "come back in 4 hours". New problems come because the city grows, never because the player was away.

### Mission complete (Tp, L5, 10 Nov)

After every won challenge, before the city screen:
1. A yellow bus pulls up in that part of the city and the hero you built gets off.
2. The heading says **"Mission complete!"**
3. The fix plays out around the hero: tarmac rolls, lamps switch on one by one, a mast blinks on, people and buses come back. 4 to 6 seconds.
4. The camera pulls back to the city view and the Booming meter fills by one step.

Tap anywhere to skip to the end. "Reduce motion" shows the end picture with no movement. The words are real text, so screen readers read them.

Exact words (one line under the heading, 40 characters or fewer for 320 x 240):
- Potholes: "Mission complete!" / "New road here. Buses are moving again."
- Lights out: "Mission complete!" / "The lamps are on. The street is awake."
- Slow internet: "Mission complete!" / "Fast internet here. Shops are busy."
- Go-slow (hall): "Mission complete!" / "Traffic lights on. The cars can move."
- Not enough yet (only if question 3 is answered b): "Mission complete!" / "Halfway there: Power 6 of 10."
- Small text at the bottom: "Tap to skip".

### The Booming meter (Tp, L7, 10 Nov)

A bar at the top of the city screen with the word **"Booming"** and a small rising-building icon. It is in steps, not colour: each fix fills one step. At set marks Lagos grows: new buildings rise, the skyline gets busier, more people and buses, and new needs show up as new pins. It never goes down by itself.

Exact words:
- Label on the bar: "Booming" and under it "Next boom: 2 fixes".
- At a mark (a short card, tap to close): "Lagos is booming!" / "New buildings are going up. More people are coming, and they need more." Button "See Lagos".
- Screen readers: "Booming meter. 1 of 3 fixes to the next boom."
- Slice 3 marks: the first boom after 3 fixes (this also turns Lagos's cog on Nigeria's wheel). Later marks come further apart.

The meter replaces the old "Lagos: 1,200 new people" counter (question 4).

### The challenge road (draft, for Tp to say yes or no: question 1)

A second way in, for players who like a clear path. A button "Challenge road" on the city screen opens a winding road of Lagos bus stops:
- Done stops are ticked ("Alausa ✓").
- The next stop glows and gently bobs, with its challenge name ("Run Computer Village").
- Later stops show a lock and "Coming soon".
- A sign along the road: "Fixes until the ferry: 3". The ferry is the end of this stretch: a jetty that gets built at the next boom (no real terminal name).
- Bus stop names are only names that pass the Q22 rule (slice 3: Alausa, Computer Village, Ikeja).
- It replaces the plain "Challenges" list and reads to screen readers as a list ("Alausa, done. Computer Village, next. Ikeja, locked.").
- Our own look: a road with danfo-yellow stop signs, not a board-game path, not a world map with paths and dots like any famous platform game (R8).

### Fix, switch on, build

| Word | When | Example of the visible change |
|---|---|---|
| **Fix** | Something broken is repaired | Potholes filled, fresh tarmac and lane paint; a drain cleared |
| **Switch on** | Power or light comes back | Lamps and shop signs light up, Computer Village signs glow (made-up shop words, never a brand) |
| **Build** | Something new the growing city needs | A bus stop, more yellow buses, a footbridge, a phone mast (no network's name or colours), a ferry jetty, a skills hub |

Never "conquer", "defeat" or "beat Lagos". A spot can take two or three steps, each with its own change.

### It never ends

1. **Lagos booms.** Each fix fills the Booming meter; at each mark Lagos grows and new needs appear.
2. **Lagos's cog turns** at the first boom. The cog keeps turning; Lagos keeps booming after it.
3. **The next state opens** on Nigeria's wheel, with its own city, real and proud sights and foods, the same everyday problems (no state is worse or to blame) and its own challenges. The next regions are the north and the east (R5), so Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba get an equal share over the updates.
4. **After all 37 cogs turn,** the hub spins (the big party), and every state keeps getting new "build" jobs. Then West Africa, as friendly neighbours.
5. Development never ends, and never goes backwards.

### Words

- Arrivals are good news. "More people are coming to Lagos!" Never "too many people" or any line that blames newcomers, a group, a job, a region or an accent. The problem is always the thing, never the people.
- No area is shown as dirty, dangerous or behind. Every area has the same kinds of problems and ends proud (Q22 rule 4).
- Real place names go only in challenge names ("Run Alausa"), bus stops and praise. A line that says something is wrong says "here" or "this road", never the place name (pending decision line 19). This matters most for Alausa (design S2.6 "Alausa, two checks").
- No real place is ever named in a line that says it is unsafe (2E).
- No real people, officials, parties, party colours, brands or government buildings anywhere.
- The player is a worker who gets things working. No mayor, no chairman, no "boss of the city".

## 2. Challenge types

### 2A. Street run (side view) and Go-Slow Hall: kept as they are

- Streets 1 to 3 (Alausa, Computer Village, Ikeja) and Go-Slow Hall stay as built and accepted in slice 2.
- You reach them from a pin (or the challenge road) instead of only the Streets list. The end card's big button is "Take it home".
- The hall's win fixes the junction: its traffic lights switch on in the Mission complete walk-in.
- Hall Win line: "You cleared General Go-Slow with {lives} lives left. You got ₦15 for Lagos." (N2.)

### 2B. The lane run (seen from behind)

Working name: **Lane run** (city choice 4 a, 10 Nov).

**The idea.** The camera sits behind the hero, a little above. Three lanes. You run forward by yourself (city choice 3 a: the one exception to "no auto-run"; streets and halls keep it) and the speed slowly rises. You run on foot past a go-slow to a bus stop. **Nothing ever drives at the hero**: vehicles are stuck or creep the same way you run.

**Phone sideways,** like every other screen. Upright shows "Turn your phone sideways to play." and pauses. A call, a switched app or a hidden tab pauses it too; play comes back with "3, 2, 1".

**Controls.**
- Swipe left or right: change lane. Swipe up: jump. Swipe down: slide. A swipe counts after 24 px in one direction within 0.3 s.
- Keys: arrows or A and D change lane, up, W or Space jump, down or S slide, P or Escape pause.
- Setting "Buttons instead of swipes": four buttons (at least 56 px): ◀ ▶ bottom left, ▲ ▼ bottom right, each with an arrow shape.

**Things in the lanes.** Three kinds, each with its own look so the player can tell them apart before they arrive (shape and outline, not colour alone):

| Kind | Things | What happens if you touch it |
|---|---|---|
| **Dodge** (solid, sharp outline) | Stuck danfo (change lane), stuck keke, parked okada with no rider, row of cones (jump), pothole (jump), barrier arm on one post (slide), low market umbrella or bunting (slide), wheelbarrow of yams (jump or change lane) | You **stumble** (a short trip, not a fall) and the Blackout gains about 15 m |
| **Burst** (wooden or loose, with a small sparkle) | Crates, junk piles, old broken signs | Run into one or land on it: it bursts with our own pop and pops out Naira, Power or Tech. No stumble |
| **Goat** | A goat crossing the road | You bounce off softly, the goat says "meeh" and trots off the road, a few kobo pop out. The goat is never hurt and you never stumble. Corn feeds it off the road (2C) |
| **Slow** | Puddle of flood water | A splash: slower for 1 s, no stumble |

People stand on the pavements as scenery only (traders at their stalls, people at bus stops, waving). People are never in the lanes and never something to dodge, hit or throw at.

**Pickups.** Coins in lines and in arcs over jumps. Power bulbs and Tech chips, fewer of them, often on the harder line. Food one at a time.

**The Blackout (Tp, L2 and L3, 10 Nov).** A creeping darkness from the Light Taker. It has no body and no face: the street lamps behind you switch off one by one, and the dark edge rolls along the road after you. It replaces lives in the lane run.
- It starts 40 m behind you and creeps about 1 m closer every 5 s.
- A stumble lets it gain about 15 m.
- Each Power pickup pushes it back 5 m (it never goes further back than 60 m).
- A garri shield takes one stumble: the Blackout does not gain.
- If it catches you, the run ends, and **you keep everything you collected**.
- So a clean run is never caught before the goal, and a first-timer can stumble twice and still be fine (3 tries rule).
- Close warning, not colour alone: under 15 m the lamps at the screen edge flicker, a low hum plays (if sound is on), and the HUD line gets a "!".
- After the goal, the Blackout creeps faster each 100 m, so the endless part ends sooner or later.

**Gentle mode in the lane run:** the Blackout starts 60 m back, gains about 10 m on a stumble, creeps half as fast, and the speed rises half as fast.

**HUD (exact words, sideways at 320 x 240):**
- Top left: three chips, each icon plus word: "₦3.40" (coin icon), "Power 6" (bulb icon), "Tech 2" (chip icon).
- Top centre: "Blackout: 40 m behind" with a small row of lamp dots that go dark as it closes. Under 15 m: "Blackout: 10 m behind!"
- Top right: "To the bus stop: 420 m". After the goal: "Best: 1,240 m".
- Pause button (at least 44 px) and the Sound button, as on the streets.

**How a run goes.**
- Everything is visible on the road for at least 1.5 s before it reaches you, at every speed. No hazard the player cannot see coming.
- The first 20 seconds of the first run teach one thing at a time with a hint word: change lane, then jump, then slide, then a crate to burst, then a goat, then a Power bulb ("Power pushes the Blackout back."), then food.
- **The goal**: reach the bus stop (600 m, about 55 s at first-timer speed). That is a win. A card asks "Keep running for more?" and the run goes on, endless and faster, until the Blackout catches you or you tap "Take it home". Being caught after the goal is still a win.
- A best distance is saved on the phone.

**What we must NOT copy** (Subway Surfers, Temple Run, Minion Rush and every other lane runner). It may play like the genre; nothing you see or hear may be theirs:
1. No trains, railway tracks, subway, tunnels, or running along the tops of vehicles.
2. **The only chaser is the Blackout**: lamps switching off behind you. No person, no guard, inspector or police, no dog or any animal, no monster, and no face, eyes or hands in the dark.
3. No "stumble once, caught the second time" rule. The Blackout works by distance in metres, shown in words.
4. No graffiti, spray cans or graffiti-style lettering.
5. No hoverboard, skateboard, jetpack, pogo stick, super sneakers or any ride that saves you. Our saving power is the garri shield.
6. No horseshoe magnet look, mystery box, score multiplier, letter hunt, keys to continue, daily missions, streaks or login prizes.
7. No yellow helper crowd, goggles, bananas, villain lab or gadgets (Minion Rush).
8. No turning corners on a cliff path and no monster chase (Temple Run).
9. Phone sideways, never an upright screen.
10. No characters, outfits, fonts, logos, menus, icons, music or sounds from any of those games. Our burst pop and pickup sounds are our own, never another game's coin or block sound.
11. Our own: the hero you built, Lagos roads, danfo, keke, okada, cones, market umbrellas, goats, a bus stop goal, Nigerian foods, our own highlife and sounds.

### 2E. Safety missions and the Shadow (Tp, L6, 10 Nov; after slice 3)

**The idea.** Some blocks go quiet because of problems: lamps out, broken bus stops, shops shut, nothing to do. Over them grows **the Shadow**: a soft, dark-blue haze, low over the empty street. It has no face, no eyes, no hands, no voice and no body; it is a problem with a shape, never a person, never a monster. Where lamps come on and people come back, it shrinks.

**The Safety Team.** A made-up neighbourhood team of everyday people, men and women, young and old, from every part of Nigeria. Our own bright vests (teal, with a white sun shape on the back and the words "SAFETY TEAM"), nothing else. No badge, cap, uniform, baton, whistle, gun or vehicle. They switch lamps on, guide people, sweep and wave. They never chase, arrest or fight anyone, and there is no one to arrest.
- Never named or coloured like a real group: not police, not the Lagos "Neighbourhood Safety Corps" (a real agency), not Amotekun, Hisbah, civil defence, LASTMA or any vigilante group.

**How a mission works.** The Shadow is a pin on a block with three steps. Each step is paid from its own bucket, and each shrinks the Shadow by a third:
1. "Switch on the lamps" (Power 10)
2. "Open a skills hub" (Tech 8): a bright room with computers and sewing machines, people learning
3. "Open a busy market" (₦20): stalls, umbrellas, food, music

After the third step, the Shadow drifts away like morning mist, the block lights up and gets a **"Safe ✓"** badge (a tick in a shield shape, plus the word).

**Where.** Only on blocks with no area name on the map. No real place is ever named in a line that says it is unsafe, and the Shadow never sits on a named area, bus stop or challenge.

**Shadow mission card (exact words):**
- Heading: "The Shadow is here."
- Line 1: "It grows where lamps are out and places are empty."
- Line 2: "Help the Safety Team bring this place back to life."
- The three steps as a list, each with its icon, cost and a tick when done: "Switch on the lamps (Power 10)", "Open a skills hub (Tech 8)", "Open a busy market (₦20)".
- Big button: the next step when you have enough ("Switch on the lamps"); if not, the challenge that helps ("Run for Power").
- Small "Back".
- Mission complete line at the end: "The Shadow is gone. This place is safe and busy again."

Not in the game: the words "crime", "criminal", "thief", "gang", "danger zone" or "area boys"; any person shown as bad; any group, job, faith or region linked to the Shadow.

## 3. How it fits a cheap phone and the 3 MB first load

All art and sound are drawn and made in code (city choice 9 a, R9 a), so they cost almost nothing to download.

1. **The city view from above is a fake 3D drawn on the 2D canvas.** Each block is drawn once into its own small picture (before and after looks), then the city is put together from these, back to front. It redraws only when you drag, zoom or something changes, never 60 times a second when still. Only blocks on screen are drawn. No WebGL, no 3D engine. Target: drag stays smooth (at least 45 fps at 4x CPU slowdown), the still city uses almost no battery.
2. **The lane run is pseudo-3D on the 2D canvas.** Road stripes narrow to the horizon; each thing is one flat picture made bigger as it comes closer. At most about 20 things on screen, reused from a pool. The Blackout is just lamps drawn dark plus one soft gradient at the back, cheap to draw. Target: 60 fps on a mid phone, never under 30 on a cheap one, at least 45 at 4x CPU.
3. **Load each challenge when first needed.** The first load holds the engine, hero, intro, city, street runs, hall and lane run. Later states download when the wheel opens them, with a note first: "Ogun is ready to download (about 400 KB)." Then they play offline.
4. **Blender** is a side test on one city picture only. If used, it makes flat pictures before the game ships (under 300 KB for the whole city, under 20 KB for each before/after block). The phone never runs 3D.
5. **First-load budget:** aim under 1.5 MB for everything in slice 3, so there is room under 3 MB. The size check in CI stays.

Six still frames drawn in code (well under 50 KB). "Skip" from the first frame; a tap moves on. Plays once before the first hero build, then lives under Settings as "Watch the story". ### Accessibility for all of it

Every button at least 44 px (in play at least 56). Sound and Music switches on the title, every pause and Settings. No meaning by colour alone: every bucket, pin, lane-run kind and badge has an icon, a word or a shape. Gentle mode everywhere. "Buttons instead of swipes". Pinch is never needed: + and − buttons zoom the city too. Screen readers hear pins as "Potholes, problem" or "New road, fixed". Reduce motion keeps the city still and skips the walk-in movement.

## 6. Original only: the check list for every new piece

On top of design R8 and part 2B's "must NOT copy" list:
1. From Homescapes, Township and Wordscapes: the loop only. No butler or helper character, no match-3 board, no letter wheel, no star currency, no farm or crops, no task list with a cost on each task, no "pick one of three styles", no population cap, no trains or zeppelins, no paid keys.
2. **The view from above (L4) is allowed, drawn our own way.** It must not look like Township or Total Battle: no diamond grid of empty plots, no drag-to-place buildings, no castle, walls, troops, army, marches or attacks on other players, no resource fields, no build clocks or speed-ups. Our town is Lagos's own shape (roads, the lagoon, blocks, yellow buses), fixed spots you repair, our bright code-drawn style.
3. From Contra: no guns, soldiers, military look, aliens, weapon letters or names.
4. From Mario and every Nintendo game: everything in R8, plus no bouncing fireballs, no fire flower, no world map with dots on a path, no blocks that pop coins when hit from below in the lane run.
5. From Subway Surfers, Temple Run and Minion Rush: part 2B's list.
6. Our own: Lagos and Nigeria, our hero, our foods, our highlife, our drawn style.
